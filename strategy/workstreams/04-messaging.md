# Workstream 04: Messaging

> **Status:** Execution brief for evidence-bounded message production and validation. No public messaging is approved by this document alone.
>
> **Evidence boundary:** The master GTM, positioning hypotheses, ICP/ABM workstream, positioning workstream, and competitive-intelligence workstream contain working hypotheses and research protocols. ## Objective

Turn approved or explicitly directional positioning into a usable message house and persona-specific message system for the Saudi B2B acquisition motion. Make every public-facing message traceable to customer language, a positioning decision, an actual capability, and approved proof before publication.

This workstream owns message architecture, message variants, proof mapping, message testing, and copy-ready guidance. It does not choose the ICP/account list, decide the market position, conduct competitive intelligence, set the offer, or invent evidence.

## Message House Production Tasks

### 1. Establish the message inventory

Create a controlled inventory of every proposed message claim, including homepage language, offer language, persona value propositions, pillar statements, proof references, objection responses, CTAs, sales talk tracks, and channel variants. Assign each item a stable ID, audience, stage, source, status, owner, and review date.

Use these statuses consistently:

- **Approved:** cleared for the named surface and audience.
- **Directional:** supported enough for testing, not public use.
- **Hypothesis:** a proposition requiring research or a message test.
- **Blocked:** contradicted, unowned, unapproved, or outside capability/proof boundaries.
- **Expired:** no longer current or past its review date.

### 2. Build the customer language bank and copy diff

- Capture exact buyer phrasing separately from analyst interpretation. Record source ID, corpus, date, speaker role, segment/trigger, consent status, and evidence label.
- Report recurring language per corpus and persona; do not merge support, reviews, sales calls, community discussion, and win/loss evidence into one undifferentiated voice.
- Flag gaps where an economic buyer, champion, operator, or blocker has no sourced language coverage.
- Compare the language bank with current or proposed copy and assign each gap one verdict: **adopt**, **bridge**, or **refuse**. A bridge teaches a strategically necessary term beside buyer language; a refuse decision records the positioning reason.
- Never synthesize a quote, smooth multiple speakers into a quote, or attribute language to a role without a source.

### 3. Produce the message house

Once positioning supplies an approved or directional frame, produce:

- Core narrative and category bridge, with evidence status.
- One core value proposition tied to the buyer's commercial job.
- Three to five message pillars, each with outcome, supporting messages, audience relevance, proof requirement, capability dependency, and forbidden overclaim.
- Differentiators expressed as buyer outcomes, not feature lists.
- Persona variations for economic buyer, champion, operator, and blocker.
- Buying-moment variants for awareness, consideration, decision, onboarding, and expansion where evidence supports them.
- Segment entry points that route to the existing leading pillar; do not create a second message house. Each segment row must use the segment map's dated trigger and confidence status.
- Objection responses that state what can be said now, what evidence is missing, and who owns the next answer.
- CTA and next-step guidance that matches the actual offer, qualification definition, and handoff contract.

The house must preserve the selected positioning decision and its alternatives. Messaging may clarify or test a frame; it may not silently promote H1, H2, or H3 to fact.

### 4. Map claims to capability and proof

For each capability claim, record the single authoritative source of truth, confirming owner, status, and boundary. Existence in documentation or configuration is not confirmation that the capability works.

Use the capability grades **WORKS**, **PARTIAL**, **LIMITS**, and **ABSENT**:

- **WORKS:** a named owner confirms the capability works as stated for the audience and tier implied.
- **PARTIAL:** it works only under a named condition such as plan, role, region, beta, or workflow; the condition must appear in copy.
- **LIMITS:** it works within a stated boundary such as scale, rate, integration, or review requirement; the boundary must appear in copy.
- **ABSENT:** the source contradicts the claim or no owner confirms it. Do not publish it; route the finding to Positioning, and to Legal/QA if it is already live.

Separately map each outcome claim to approved proof. Proof must include source, date, customer/segment context, permission, claim scope, limitations, owner, and review date. A customer outcome does not prove universal performance or product capability.

### 5. Create channel-ready packages

Adapt the approved house for website, sales enablement, email/campaign, ABM, content, events, and product-adjacent surfaces without changing the category, primary value, audience, or proof. Record surface-specific constraints, CTA, owner, approval state, and straggler-review date.

## Persona / Message Tests

### Test design rules

Test a meaningful framing or audience variation, not isolated wording trivia. Before any test, record the five-pillar diagnosis: ICP alignment, differentiation, clarity, consequence/emotion, and cross-channel consistency. Grade each weak, adequate, or strong and route the fix before testing.

Record a feasibility gate before designing the test:

- **Targetability:** the channel can reach the intended role and segment with acceptable contamination.
- **Qualified volume:** the channel can produce enough in-scope interactions in the decision window to inform the question.
- **Measurement reach:** instrumentation can observe the intended signal beyond attention, including reply quality, meeting quality, objection, or opportunity progression.

If the gate fails, use structured qualitative research and directional signals. Do not declare a winner from underpowered noise. For a powered paid test, defer the statistical verdict to `paid-media-creative-strategist`.

| Audience / moment | Test question | Evidence to collect | Status / owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer / awareness | Does the bounded commercial outcome and accountability frame describe a live priority? | Unprompted problem recognition, consequence, credibility, next-step intent | `[NEEDS INPUT]` / Messaging + Insights |
| Economic buyer / decision | Does the message reduce perceived investment, execution, or vendor risk without promising an outcome? | Proof requested, decision criteria, objections, approval path | `[NEEDS INPUT]` / Messaging + Sales |
| Champion / consideration | Does one accountable operating partner feel more useful than coordinating alternatives? | Alternative recognition, coordination burden, trust, meeting quality | `[NEEDS INPUT]` / Messaging + Positioning |
| Operator / consideration | Are inputs, approvals, handoff, and weekly responsibilities clear? | Five-second comprehension, workflow usability, missing-input objections | `[NEEDS INPUT]` / Messaging + Ops |

| Arabic/English or bilingual role variant | Does the variant preserve meaning and sound natural to the intended role? | Qualified human review, comprehension, objection, consent | `[NEEDS INPUT]` / Messaging + reviewer |

### Composite verdict

A message is **validated** only when a majority of mixed qualitative and quantitative signals support it and the evidence limitations are recorded. Use **not validated** when the evidence contradicts it. Use **not yet** when signals are incomplete, underpowered, or split. No single click, reply, preference, or anecdote is sufficient.

## Proof Boundary

The following distinctions are mandatory:

- **Customer language:** proves how a sourced buyer described a problem or outcome; it does not prove the claim is broadly true.
- **Positioning evidence:** supports a category, alternative, or buyer frame; it does not prove the product can deliver it.
- **Capability evidence:** confirms what the actual service can do and under which conditions; only a human-confirmed source can grade a capability WORKS.
- **Outcome proof:** supports a named result in a defined context; it does not justify a universal, average, benchmark, or forecast claim.
- **Competitive intelligence:** shows an observed or reported alternative, provider claim, objection, or perception; it does not establish competitor effectiveness.
- **Local relevance:** requires segment-specific evidence and qualified review; geography, language, relationships, or trust must not be used as decorative proof.
- **AI language:** must state the human review and data/control boundary where relevant; do not imply autonomy, proprietary technology, faster results, lower cost, or better performance without approved evidence.
- **Public use:** requires the applicable positioning decision, capability owner, proof/permission owner, legal/QA review, and surface owner to be recorded.

No invented claim, metric, customer result, logo, testimonial, quote, market fact, competitor assessment, local-access assertion, or performance statement may enter the message house as proof.

## Acceptance Criteria

The workstream is accepted for the next gate when:

- A named owner, approval path, review cadence, and message inventory exist.
- The message house uses the current positioning decision and does not duplicate or override ICP/ABM, positioning, or competitive-intelligence ownership.
- Every headline, pillar, persona value proposition, and material capability claim is mapped to a source, status, owner, and review date.
- Every customer-language entry is attributable, dated, role-tagged, consent-aware, and kept distinct from analyst interpretation; no synthesized quote exists.
- Every capability claim has a named internal source of truth and human confirmation grade; documentation or a symbol alone never grades a claim WORKS.
- PARTIAL and LIMITS claims state their condition or boundary; ABSENT claims are blocked and routed to Positioning and, where live, Legal/QA.
- Every outcome claim has approved proof or is visibly marked directional, hypothesis, or `[NEEDS INPUT]`; no invented metric, quote, testimonial, logo, or result appears.
- H1/H2/H3 language remains labeled according to the positioning decision and is not silently promoted to validated positioning.
- Persona and segment variants preserve the same message house; segment variants use a dated trigger, one leading pillar, confidence status, and the swap/merge test where applicable.
- Each cleared message test includes the five-pillar diagnosis, pre-test feasibility gate, test design, evidence limitations, and composite verdict; underpowered quantitative evidence is not called a winner.
- High-priority surfaces have been compared pairwise on category, primary value, audience, and proof, with contradictions assigned to owners.
- Downstream recipients accept their handoffs, and the feedback loop includes a source, owner, due date, escalation trigger, and next review.
- Public-use approval is recorded only after positioning, capability, proof/permission, legal/QA, and surface-owner checks pass.
