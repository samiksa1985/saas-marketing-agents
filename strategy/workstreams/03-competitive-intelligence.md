# Workstream 03: Competitive Intelligence

> **Status:** Execution research protocol. Not validated market research, a competitor ranking, or public-facing proof.
>
> **Service context:** Saudi-focused AI-native outsourced customer acquisition department for B2B SMEs and mid-market companies.
>
> **Evidence boundary:** Do not invent competitor names, market statistics, pricing, customer proof, capabilities, performance, local access, regulatory expertise, or buyer claims. Until sourced and approved, record a statement as `[NEEDS INPUT]`, `Unknown`, or `Hypothesis`.

## 1. Purpose and decision frame

This workstream determines what a target buyer would actually choose instead of the proposed service, why they would choose it, and what evidence is needed to make a defensible comparison. The unit of analysis is the buyer's acquisition job and decision process, not a list of companies or features.

The working service hypothesis is an outsourced B2B acquisition department that coordinates buyer and message research, targeting, engagement, qualification, nurture, sales handoff, and measurement around one commercial objective. AI is a proposed operating mechanism; human judgment, local context, approvals, claims, privacy, and customer-facing decisions remain explicit control points.

The primary positioning hypothesis is H1: **outsourced B2B acquisition department**. H2, **AI-native pipeline operating partner**, is a message/category test. H3, **Saudi market-entry and demand validation partner**, is a trigger-specific test for genuine launch or expansion situations. None is validated.

### Decisions this protocol must inform

1. Which alternatives belong in the first comparison set for each segment and trigger.
2. Which buyer outcomes and risks distinguish the alternatives in practice.
3. Which differentiation claims the actual delivery model can sustain.
4. Which objections, proof requests, and loss conditions belong in messaging and sales enablement.
5. Whether the first offer should be framed as a bounded acquisition sprint, a department mandate, or a trigger-specific validation engagement.
6. Which intelligence is urgent enough to communicate within 48 hours and which belongs in the periodic library.

### Non-decisions

This workstream does not estimate market size or share, declare a market leader, publish a competitor score, copy competitor creative, infer advertising performance, or set pricing without approved evidence. Advertising presence indicates a funded message or test, not effectiveness. Absence from a public ad library is unknown, not proof of inactivity.

## 2. Research scope and operating rules

### Required comparison set

Research the alternative actually available to the buyer. A named vendor is optional; a behavior or status quo is not. Start with these six tracks and add a track only when interview or account evidence shows it is a real decision path:

| Track | What to investigate | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal hire** | Continuing founder/sales ownership; hiring one role; assembling a team; hiring a department; internal use of existing staff. Capture trigger, hiring threshold, time-to-productivity expectation, management burden, and capability gaps. | That internal staff is slower, cheaper, better, or more trusted. |
| **Fragmented vendors** | Freelancers, specialist providers, channel-specific vendors, consultants, and the coordination between them. Capture scope boundaries, handoffs, reporting, duplication, gaps, and client coordination effort. | That several specialists necessarily produce poor accountability. |
| **Generalist agency** | Broad agency engagement, campaign-led scope, reporting model, sales handoff, strategic ownership, and renewal/exit conditions. | That generalist means low quality or stops at activity. |
| **Referrals / partners** | Referrals, founder relationships, associations, distributors, resellers, events, tenders, community routes, and existing customer networks. Capture access, trust, control, repeatability, timing, and dependency on individuals. | That relationship-led acquisition is unscalable or less measurable. |
| **Software / tooling** | CRM, marketing automation, prospecting, enrichment, advertising, analytics, AI, and workflow tools used by the buyer or their team. Capture the job solved, human work still required, adoption, governance, and integration burden. | That software replaces ownership, strategy, selling, or delivery. |
| **Local provider** | Saudi-based or locally positioned providers where the buyer considers local presence, language, trust, contracting, sector familiarity, or access relevant. Capture the specific evidence behind each perceived advantage. | That “local” means more relevant, compliant, connected, or effective. |

The same buyer may use several alternatives together. Record the bundle and the switching or coordination decision rather than forcing one category.

### Source mix

Use at least two independent source types before treating a pattern as recurring:

- Buyer and non-buyer interviews, including users and rejectors of each alternative.
- Recent proposals, scopes, briefs, procurement documents, renewal/exit notes, and anonymized loss records where permission exists.
- Public provider websites, service pages, documentation, job postings, public case material, official pricing pages, and terms.
- Product trials, demos, official documentation, and observed workflows where access is lawful and authorized.
- Customer review platforms and public discussion, recorded as perception evidence rather than objective product truth.
- Official public ad libraries relevant to the provider, checked at each profile refresh or launch signal. Summarize message, offer, CTA, audience/geography signal, first-seen date, last-seen date, and creative-volume change. Never reproduce ad copy verbatim.
- Internal capability and delivery records for the proposed service. Internal belief is not external proof.

Do not use scraped personal data, restricted material, confidential customer information, purchased lists, or unverified sales assertions as standalone evidence. Confirm public claims against a primary source where possible.

### Evidence labels

Every record uses one label:

- **Observed:** Directly seen, heard, or received; preserve exact source and date.
- **Reported:** A person or document states it; identify the source and do not upgrade it to fact.
- **Inferred:** Analyst interpretation supported by one or more records.
- **Hypothesis:** A proposition to test.
- **Unknown:** Not found, inaccessible, or not yet verified.
- **Approved proof:** Evidence cleared for external use by the named owner.

## 3. Evidence capture protocol

### Minimum record

Create one record per evidence item in the competitive intelligence ledger. Do not combine unrelated observations into a polished conclusion.

| Field | Required capture |
|---|---|
| Record ID | Stable ID, date captured, researcher, status. |
| Alternative track | One or more of the six tracks; note bundled alternatives. |
| Buyer context | Segment, sector if known, company stage/size only when sourced, role, trigger, geography, and buying stage. |
| Source | Interview ID, document, URL, public library, demo, review, CRM/loss record, or internal capability record. |
| Source date | Publication, observation, interview, first-seen, last-seen, or transaction date as applicable. |
| Evidence text | Short paraphrase or controlled note. Preserve verbatim buyer language separately with consent; never reproduce competitor ad creative. |
| Evidence label | Observed, Reported, Inferred, Hypothesis, Unknown, or Approved proof. |
| Confidence | High, medium, or low, with the reason. Confidence describes evidence quality, not belief. |
| Recency | Current, aging, or stale against a stated review window. |
| Implication | What decision, message, test, or risk this may affect. |
| Contradiction | Conflicting evidence, unresolved interpretation, or missing source. |
| Owner / next action | Named owner, due date, and the cheapest action to resolve uncertainty. |
| Approval | Legal, privacy, brand, customer, or leadership approval required before external use. |

### Interview evidence

Record the last real decision, not a hypothetical preference. Capture the initial trigger, options considered, people involved, evaluation criteria, work performed, decision date, spend or effort only if voluntarily and reliably supplied, outcome, and what would cause a change. Separate the interviewee's words from the researcher's interpretation.

### Public provider evidence

Capture what the provider claims, where it claims it, and what is verifiable. Record category language, target segment, offer structure, proof format, workflow scope, human involvement, AI/data language, onboarding, contract signals, and calls to action. A provider claim is evidence of positioning, not evidence that the claim is true.

### Advertising signal record

For each relevant official library and tracked provider, record:

| Field | Required capture |
|---|---|
| Library and provider identifier | Official source and search method. |
| Check date | Date and region/account settings used. |
| Live message pattern | Category-level summary of promise, offer, audience, and CTA; no verbatim creative. |
| First seen / last seen | Library dates when available; otherwise mark Unknown. |
| Volume change | New, stable, reduced, or unknown versus the prior check, with the observation basis. |
| Signal | Possible launch, ICP, geographic, or funnel-stage movement. Label as Hypothesis until corroborated. |
| Announcement comparison | Later public announcement date, if any, and appeared-versus-announced gap. |
| Limits | Library coverage, date lag, region limits, and what cannot be inferred. |

## 4. Comparison dimensions

Use the dimensions below across every alternative, but allow “not applicable” and “unknown.” Compare by buyer job and outcome, not by raw feature count.

1. **Problem fit:** Which acquisition problem does the option address, and which does it leave with the client?
2. **Accountability:** Named owner, decision rights, cadence, escalation, and responsibility for sales handoff.
3. **Commercial continuity:** Connection from FIND through CONVERT: research, targeting, engagement, qualification, nurture, handoff, and measurement.
4. **Buyer and offer understanding:** Ability to use customer expertise, define a clear offer, map roles, and preserve buyer language.
5. **Execution scope:** What is included, excluded, client-owned, or dependent on another provider.
6. **Speed and learning loop:** Time to start, feedback cadence, experiment design, and how decisions change after evidence.
7. **Quality and human judgment:** Review of claims, personalization, Arabic/English work, local nuance, and customer-facing output.
8. **Data, privacy, and governance:** Data access, retention, model use, consent, security material, approvals, and auditability. Legal must verify obligations.
9. **Trust and proof:** References, relevant outcomes, independent reviews, provider transparency, and proof approval status. Unknown is not a weakness score.
10. **Operational burden:** Client time, coordination load, approvals, sales response, onboarding, and internal capability required.
11. **Reach and access:** Buyer access, channel permissions, partner dependencies, procurement readiness, and geographic or language coverage when evidenced.
12. **Flexibility and lock-in:** Ability to test one offer and one motion, change scope, own data/assets, and exit without stranded work.
13. **Economics:** Capture price, effort, cost structure, minimum term, discount, or payback only when sourced. Do not estimate or normalize missing values.
14. **Failure conditions:** What makes the option unsuitable, including no clear offer, no owner, no follow-up, no delivery capacity, or a demand for guarantees.
15. **Buyer language:** Exact recurring phrases for the job, risk, desired outcome, and objection, with source and consent status.

### Comparison discipline

Do not create a numeric score until the team has agreed definitions, evidence thresholds, and missing-data treatment. A blank cell means unknown, not zero. If a scorecard is later approved, publish the evidence behind every score and keep source, date, segment, and confidence visible.

## 5. Interview protocol

### Sampling requirements

Recruit across the working segments in the GTM context: founder-led specialist B2B firms, commercially ready mid-market units, new offer/market-entry units, and capacity-constrained service operators. Include current users, recent rejectors, people who chose another route, and people who delayed. Record role, segment, trigger, and alternative used; do not invent a quota or claim representativeness before a sampling plan is approved.

### Core questions

1. Tell me about the last time the company needed more qualified commercial conversations. What triggered action?
2. What did you actually do first, and what other options did you consider?
3. Did you keep acquisition internal, hire, use several providers, use one agency, rely on referrals/partners, buy software, use a local provider, or wait? Why?
4. Who owned the decision, who did the work, and where did responsibility move between marketing, sales, leadership, and delivery?
5. What did “qualified” mean in that decision? Which inquiries or leads were not useful?
6. What work still had to happen after a provider, partner, or tool generated a signal?
7. Which part of the process was hardest to coordinate, verify, or measure?
8. What evidence did you request before approving the option? Which proof did you trust, and which did not help?
9. What made the chosen option feel lower risk than the alternatives?
10. What disappointed you, created rework, or caused you to stop, renew, switch, or delay?
11. How much access, approval time, sales follow-up, customer knowledge, or delivery capacity did the option require from your team?
12. Which language, relationship, procurement, contracting, privacy, or data-handling requirements affected the decision? What was specific to this context?
13. What would make an outsourced acquisition department more useful than an internal hire, several specialists, a generalist agency, a partner route, software, or a local provider?
14. What would make it feel more risky or less credible?
15. If AI supported research, production, routing, follow-up, or analysis, which decisions would require human review before customer contact?
16. What would you need to see after a bounded first engagement to scale, revise, or stop?
17. May we use your exact wording internally? May we use it externally? Record consent separately; never turn an interview statement into a public testimonial without approval.

### Follow-up prompts

Ask “What happened next?”, “How did you know?”, “Who saw that evidence?”, “What did it cost in time or coordination?”, and “Can you show me the artifact or workflow?” Avoid leading prompts that introduce the proposed category or desired answer.

## 6. Output format

Maintain one controlled intelligence library with these artifacts:

### A. Alternative evidence ledger

One row per record using the minimum fields above. Include source links, dates, evidence labels, confidence, contradictions, and approval state.

### B. Alternative profile

For each track, produce a short profile with:

- Buyer job and trigger observed.
- Typical workflow as evidenced, including client-owned work.
- Decision participants and approval risks.
- Strengths, limitations, and failure conditions by segment.
- Evidence-backed language and objections.
- Unknowns and next research actions.
- Implications for H1, H2, and H3.

Do not write a competitor dossier for a provider until a named provider is sourced and the evidence boundary allows it.

### C. Comparison matrix

Compare the proposed service and alternatives against the approved dimensions. Use `Observed`, `Reported`, `Inferred`, `Hypothesis`, or `Unknown` in cells where a conclusion is not yet proven. Include the segment and trigger for every row.

### D. Objection and proof register

| Objection / proof request | Buyer role and context | Alternative behind it | Evidence | Response boundary | Owner / status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What the buyer actually said or asked | Role, segment, trigger, date | Option being compared | Source ID and evidence label | What can be said now; what is `[NEEDS INPUT]` | Owner, approval, next test |

### E. Monthly change log

Log only sourced changes: new positioning language, offer or packaging change, public capability change, hiring signal, partnership signal, product release, pricing publication/change, review trend, or ad-activity signal. Record detected date, source date, source, confidence, implications, and handoff status. Treat uncorroborated signals as hypotheses.

### F. Decision memo

At each gate, summarize the decision, alternatives compared, strongest evidence, contradictions, unknowns, message implications, capability gaps, and the explicit decision: continue, test, revise, park, or stop. Include what changed since the prior memo.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

The workstream is ready for the next GTM gate only when:

- All six required alternative tracks have an owner, scope, source plan, and explicit unknowns.
- Each included account or interview has segment, role, trigger, alternative used, date, source, and consent/permission status where relevant.
- Evidence is labeled and traceable; no unsupported claim is presented as a fact.
- At least two independent source types are used for any pattern labeled recurring, unless the record is explicitly marked provisional.
- The comparison uses the approved dimensions and distinguishes provider claims, buyer perception, observed workflow, and approved proof.
- The proposed service is compared against the buyer's actual bundled status quo, not only a named provider.
- Interview notes include recent behavior, decision participants, alternatives, evidence requested, failure conditions, and next step; hypothetical preference alone is insufficient.
- Public ad-library checks, where relevant, record message pattern, CTA, dates, volume change, coverage limits, and the fact that effectiveness is unknowable.
- No competitor creative is reproduced verbatim, and no confidential or restricted data is stored in the library.
- Every downstream handoff has an owner, acceptance criteria, unresolved assumptions, and feedback loop.
- Legal/privacy, capability, proof, and brand approvals are marked before any comparison claim is used externally.
- The decision memo states what remains unknown and chooses continue, test, revise, park, or stop.

### Gate alignment

| GTM gate | Competitive intelligence evidence required |
|---|---|
| **Day 10 list gate** | Alternative map, account/interview source quality, ownership, suppression, and research confidence. |
| **Day 30 signal gate** | Buyer comprehension, alternative recognition, trigger strength, objections, and evidence quality. |
| **Day 60 economics gate** | Sourced effort and coordination burden, proposal objections, delivery implications, and mature cohort evidence where available. |
| **Day 90 continuation gate** | Repeatable wedge, credible proof path, defensible differentiation, service capacity implications, and approved compliance posture. |

## 10. First 14 days

| Day | Action | Deliverable / decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Appoint workstream owner and confirm the evidence boundary. | Owner, working log, approved labels, storage location. |
| 2 | Convert H1, H2, and H3 into a claim and test register. | Claim, evidence needed, owner, status, and falsification check for each hypothesis. |
| 3 | Create the alternative ledger with the six required tracks and bundled-status-quo field. | Empty but controlled ledger with stable IDs and source taxonomy. |
| 4 | Confirm ICP segments, triggers, roles, exclusions, and suppression rules from the master GTM. | Research brief and account/interview inclusion rules. |
| 5 | Gather existing internal artifacts: proposals, loss notes, delivery workflows, capability records, and approved proof. | Internal evidence pack; mark gaps and permissions. |
| 6 | Draft the interview screener and consent language. | Approved screener covering recent decisions, alternatives, evidence, and failure conditions. |
| 7 | Recruit the first interview set across users, rejectors, delayed buyers, and alternative users. | Scheduled interviews with context fields completed. |
| 8 | Review public sources for each known or newly sourced provider and relevant official ad libraries. | Source log; category-level ad observations only; no verbatim creative. |
| 9 | Run initial interviews and capture exact buyer language separately from interpretation. | Interview records and same-day open questions. |
| 10 | Hold the Day 10 list gate with Insights, Positioning, ABM, Ops, Legal/Privacy, and Coordinator. | Approve, narrow, or reject research/account set; record decision. |
| 11 | Populate first alternative profiles and contradiction log. | Six-track profiles with unknowns and next evidence actions. |
| 12 | Build the first comparison matrix and objection/proof register. | Evidence-linked matrix; no numeric ranking unless approved. |
| 13 | Run a message comprehension check comparing H1, H2, and H3 against the actual alternatives. | Directional clarity, trust, differentiation, and risk notes; label sample limits. |
| 14 | Produce the first decision memo and downstream handoff pack. | Continue/test/revise/park/stop decision, owners, acceptance criteria, and next 30-day backlog. |

### First-14-day exit condition

The workstream may proceed into live positioning and acquisition tests only when the Day 10 gate is passed, the evidence ledger is traceable, the first comparison matrix distinguishes unknowns from facts, interview and public-source limits are recorded, and every external claim has an owner and approval path. Otherwise, narrow the research question and continue discovery.
