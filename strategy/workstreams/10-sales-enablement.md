# Workstream 10: Sales Enablement

> **Status:** Execution brief for selling and delivering the first paid Pipeline Reset and Acquisition Sprint. It is not approved pricing, proof, legal language, or a public claims library.
>
> **Evidence boundary:** Upstream artifacts contain working hypotheses and research protocols. Do not present their positioning, customer language, local relevance, capability, performance, pricing, or outcome assumptions as facts. Use `[NEEDS INPUT]`, `Unknown`, or `Hypothesis` until the accountable owner supplies and approves the evidence.

## Objective

Equip the commercial owner to run a disciplined buyer conversation, decide whether an account is suitable for the first paid **Pipeline Reset and Acquisition Sprint**, present a bounded proposal or pilot, handle material objections honestly, and transfer an accepted engagement into delivery without losing the buyer's problem, evidence, owners, or decision criteria.

The sprint is a trust-building test of operating fit around one offer, one buyer segment, one primary acquisition motion, and one commercial objective. It is not a promise of leads, revenue, speed, local access, AI performance, or guaranteed outcomes. The sales enablement workstream owns the conversation system and handoff quality; it does not invent the offer, set price, approve claims, interpret law, or replace delivery ownership.

**Decision authority:**

- The commercial owner approves the actual offer, price, terms, scope, commitments, and any external claim.
- Delivery confirms what can be executed and accepts or rejects the proposed scope.
- Legal/Privacy decides legal, privacy, data, contracting, and channel questions; Sales Enablement must not infer approval.
- Marketing Ops / Measurement owns CRM fields, stages, attribution, and reporting definitions.

No proposal or paid pilot may be sent with an unknown owner, unsupported claim, unapproved price, unresolved material data issue, or absent next-step owner.

## Conversation Contract

Every sales conversation must answer five questions before a proposal is recommended:

1. **Why this account, why now?** Sourced ICP fit, trigger, timing, and current commercial objective.
2. **What is actually broken or constrained?** Buyer-described problem, current process, implication, and evidence, separated from the seller's hypothesis.
3. **Who must decide and who must deliver?** Economic buyer, champion/commercial owner, operator, blockers, procurement, privacy/technical reviewer, and client-side follow-up owner.
4. **What would make the sprint worth doing?** Agreed learning or commercial decision, baseline, success evidence, responsibilities, and scale/revise/stop criteria.
5. **What happens next?** Specific action, owner, date, required inputs, and acceptance of the next meeting or proposal step.

A meeting, positive reply, content interaction, or request for pricing is an engagement signal. None is a qualified opportunity by itself.

## Discovery Guide

### Pre-call readiness

Before the call, the seller reviews the ABM record and records:

- Account ID, ICP segment/tier, B2B offer evidence, source, dated trigger, corroboration, and confidence.
- Contact role and likely committee position; mark missing roles as unknown rather than guessing.
- Current alternative or status quo hypothesis: internal ownership, fragmented providers, generalist agency, referrals/partners, software/tooling, local provider, or other sourced route.
- One problem hypothesis and one relevant sprint conversation angle, both labeled `Hypothesis` until confirmed.
- Suppression/permission status, channel eligibility, language-review need, and paired seller.
- `[NEEDS INPUT: approved pre-call research sources, account brief template, and time available for preparation]`.

Do not prepare a bespoke proposal, ROI claim, competitor score, or customer story before the buyer confirms the problem and decision context.

### Call opening

Use a permission-based opening and set a mutual agenda:

> “Thanks for making the time. I’d like to understand how you currently create, qualify, and follow up on commercial demand, what prompted the conversation, and whether a bounded sprint could be useful. I’ll also explain what we can and cannot take on once I understand the situation. If there is no fit, we can say that plainly. Does that agenda work?”

The seller should keep the buyer talking for most of the call, use the buyer's own language in notes, and avoid leading with AI or a full-service menu.

### Discovery sequence

| Area | Questions | Evidence to capture |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial context | “What offer or business line are you trying to grow?” “Which buyer or segment matters for this objective?” | Defined B2B offer, segment, owner, delivery capacity, and objective. |
| Trigger and timing | “What changed or made this worth discussing now?” “What happens if the current approach continues?” | Dated trigger, urgency, consequence, and decision window. |
| Current process | “How do accounts currently move from first signal to a sales conversation?” “Where does ownership change?” | Current motion, channels, handoffs, follow-up, CRM/process, and client-owned work. |
| Problem | “Where does momentum or quality get lost?” “Which conversations are not useful, and why?” | Specific acquisition, qualification, follow-up, booking, or measurement problem; buyer language. |
| Implication | “Who feels this problem?” “What does it prevent the team from doing?” “How do you know?” | Operational, commercial, and strategic implications; source and confidence. Do not invent cost or revenue. |
| Baseline | “What can we inspect from recent leads, proposals, customers, or sales outcomes?” “Which definitions do you already use?” | Available evidence, baseline window, data quality, missing fields, and measurement constraints. |
| Desired future state | “What would be different after a useful test?” “Which decision should the sprint help you make?” | Observable learning or progression decision, not an unsupported outcome promise. |
| Buying process | “How do you evaluate a provider like this?” “What approvals and procurement steps are required?” | Decision criteria, process, paper process, budget owner, timeline, and blockers. |
| Stakeholders | “Who owns the commercial result?” “Who will review messages, data, Arabic work, privacy, or delivery?” | Economic buyer, champion, operator, blockers, and missing committee coverage. |
| Client commitment | “Who can provide customer/prospect context, approve work, and follow up with buyers each week?” | Named client owners, access, approval cadence, response responsibility, and capacity. |
| Alternatives | “What else are you considering: internal hire, specialists, agency, partners, software, or waiting?” “Why?” | Actual alternative bundle, evaluation criteria, perceived risk, and loss condition. |
| Close | “Based on this, should we disqualify, continue discovery, design a sprint, or schedule a decision meeting?” | Agreed disposition, next action, owner, date, and required inputs. |

### Role prompts

Use only the prompts relevant to the participant. Do not assume the title means the person owns the decision.

- **Founder / CEO / GM:** “Which commercial objective has executive attention?” “What would make an external operating partner lower risk than hiring or waiting?”
- **Commercial / Sales leader:** “Where does demand become unusable for sales?” “What response, qualification, and handoff discipline would your team actually adopt?”
- **Marketing / Operator:** “Which inputs and approvals are currently missing?” “What would create coordination work rather than remove it?”
- **Finance / Procurement:** “What must be clear about scope, payment, evidence, data, and exit before approval?”
- **IT / Privacy / Operations:** “Which data may be used, by whom, for what purpose, and under what review controls?” “What would block onboarding or customer-facing execution?”

## Qualification Rubric

Score only what discovery supports. A missing answer is `Unknown`, not zero evidence of disinterest. The score organizes follow-up; it does not replace judgment or the stated exit criteria.

| Dimension | 0: absent or blocked | 1: partial / unverified | 2: confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICP and offer fit | No clear B2B offer, anti-ICP, or delivery mismatch | Fit appears plausible but evidence or segment is incomplete | Defined B2B offer, relevant segment, and delivery capacity are documented |
| Problem severity | No specific problem or only generic interest | Problem described but impact, owner, or process is unclear | Specific problem is buyer-confirmed and affects a named commercial workflow |
| Trigger and timing | No current reason or evaluation is remote | Trigger exists but timing or consequence is unclear | Dated trigger and credible decision window are recorded |
| Evidence access | No usable baseline or unwilling to share required context | Some records exist but completeness is unknown | Agreed baseline/process evidence, access owner, and data limits are documented |
| Authority and committee | No plausible economic buyer or access path | Champion exists but authority/committee is incomplete | Economic buyer, champion, required reviewers, and access path are known |
| Client operating readiness | No follow-up, approvals, delivery capacity, or owner | Owner exists but cadence/capacity is unconfirmed | Named owners can provide inputs, approvals, follow-up, and delivery capacity |
| Solution and capability fit | Sprint would require unsupported scope or guarantees | Fit is plausible but capability/AI/data boundary needs confirmation | Proposed sprint maps to actual approved capability and clear exclusions |
| Commercial process | No budget path, decision process, or next step | Process exists but price/terms/approvals are unresolved | Buying process, commercial owner, next decision, and date are clear |
| Risk and alternative fit | Material legal, privacy, procurement, or alternative risk is blocked | Risk is known with an owner but unresolved | Risks and alternatives have a documented response or acceptance path |

**Disposition guidance:**

- **Proceed to sprint design:** all critical dimensions are confirmed or have a named resolution owner, with no material blocker; the seller has an agreed proposal-design next step.
- **Continue discovery:** the problem and timing are credible, but one or more critical dimensions remain unresolved and the buyer has committed to provide the missing evidence or stakeholder access.
- **Nurture / revisit:** relevant problem or fit exists, but timing, authority, evidence access, capacity, or approval path is not current. Record a dated revisit condition.
- **Disqualify / decline:** no fit, no credible problem, unsupported requested outcome, no client owner, no delivery capacity, or a material blocker the engagement cannot responsibly resolve.

## Proposal / Pilot Narrative

The proposal should be a buyer-specific decision document, not a generic capability deck. Use this sequence:

1. **Their situation:** repeat the buyer's confirmed objective, current process, trigger, and problem in their language. Label any interpretation.
2. **The decision to make:** state what the bounded sprint will help the buyer learn or decide. Do not promise a revenue result.
3. **The proposed scope:** one offer, one segment, one primary motion, research/message work, qualification definition, sales handoff, follow-up, and measurement only as actually approved. `[NEEDS INPUT: final scope]`.
4. **Operating model:** name provider and client owners, approvals, weekly access, data inputs, response expectations, and escalation path.
5. **Evidence plan:** define baseline, account/source records, funnel stages, cohort window, instrumentation, and what will remain unknown. Follow the measurement system's `FIND` through `CONVERT` definitions.
6. **Decision gate:** pre-agree the evidence that would support **scale**, **revise**, or **stop**. Do not import generic benchmarks.
7. **Alternatives and risks:** address the buyer's actual internal, vendor, partner, software, or status-quo alternative. Keep unknown comparison cells unknown.
8. **Commercial terms:** insert only approved price, duration, payment terms, scope limits, and contract language. `[NEEDS INPUT: approved commercial terms]`.
9. **Next step:** specify the decision meeting, required attendees, inputs, owner, and date.

### Pilot narrative language

> “The proposed first engagement is a bounded test around one commercial objective. We would agree the account or buyer scope, the operating responsibilities, the evidence we can inspect, and the decision criteria before work begins. The point is to learn whether this motion and operating model fit your situation, then decide together whether to scale, revise, or stop. It is not a guarantee of leads, revenue, timing, or performance.”

Replace this only after the commercial owner approves the actual scope and Legal approves any customer-facing language.

### Proposal acceptance gate

Before sending, verify: buyer-confirmed problem; named economic buyer/champion; approved offer and price; scope and exclusions; client responsibilities; evidence and data boundaries; legal/privacy status; proof status; alternative and objection coverage; success/decision criteria; proposal expiry if approved; next meeting owner/date; CRM opportunity and source fields.

## Objection Handling

Use a four-step pattern: acknowledge the concern, ask what specifically drives it, reframe around the buyer's decision, and offer only an evidence-backed next step. Never answer an evidence gap with a fabricated proof point.

| Objection | Response frame | Required follow-up / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| “We need more leads.” | “Let’s define what a useful commercial conversation means and where the current process loses quality before choosing volume.” | Confirm qualification, current process, sales capacity, and baseline. No lead-volume promise. |
| “We can hire internally.” | “That may be the right choice if you have the role, management capacity, and time to assemble the full motion. May we compare the work you need owned and the evidence needed for each path?” | Use the internal-hire alternative only with sourced buyer criteria; do not claim faster, cheaper, or better. |
| “We already use agencies/freelancers.” | “Several specialists may be appropriate. The question is where coordination, qualification, handoff, or measurement remains client-owned.” | Map the bundle and gaps; do not disparage providers. |
| “We already get business through referrals.” | “Referrals may be the strongest route for your situation. We should understand what is working, what is dependent on individuals, and whether a second motion is actually needed.” | Never call referrals unscalable or inferior without evidence. |
| “Why not just buy software or use AI tools?” | “Tools may be useful for a defined part of the workflow. This engagement is about deciding who owns the commercial loop, judgment, approvals, follow-up, and learning.” | Provide only approved capability and data-use details; no autonomous-AI claim. |
| “How is this different from a generalist agency?” | “Let’s compare the operating job you need covered: scope, accountability, qualification, sales handoff, client effort, and measurement.” | Use Workstream 03 evidence and actual scope. Do not claim an agency stops at activity unless sourced. |
| “Do you have Saudi customers or local access?” | “That is a reasonable proof question. We can share only approved evidence. Where we do not have evidence, we should state that and define how the engagement would validate the relevant context.” | `[NEEDS INPUT: approved local proof, references, and named permission]`; never imply local access. |
| “Can you guarantee pipeline or revenue?” | “No responsible proposal should guarantee an outcome we cannot control. We can define the inputs, operating work, measurement, and decision gate we can own.” | Escalate any requested guarantee; remove unsupported performance language. |
| “What results should we expect?” | “The first step is to establish the baseline and agreed definitions. We can describe the work and decision criteria once approved; we should not import a benchmark or invent a forecast.” | `[NEEDS INPUT: approved planning range, if any]`; label all ranges as planning inputs, not claims. |
| “Your price is too high / give us a discount.” | “Let’s separate scope, client effort, evidence, risk, and commercial terms before changing price.” | `[NEEDS INPUT: approved price, discount floor, payment terms, and authority]`; do not improvise. |
| “We need a POC first.” | “A bounded paid sprint can be structured around the decision you need to make, with explicit inputs, scope, and stop criteria.” | Confirm what a POC must prove, who supplies inputs, and whether the proposed sprint actually tests it. |
| “We need legal/security/privacy approval.” | “Let’s bring the responsible reviewer into the process and document data, retention, access, model use, and customer-facing controls.” | `[NEEDS INPUT: approved security/privacy materials and legal owner]`; no certification or compliance claim. |
| “We need to involve sales/finance/our CEO.” | “That makes sense because the sprint depends on commercial ownership, approvals, and follow-up. Let’s agree who needs to join and what each person must decide.” | Multi-thread; do not treat a champion as economic buyer. |
| “Not now.” | “Understood. What would need to change for this to become timely, and when should we revisit?” | Record reason, permission/status, owner, date, and trigger; move to nurture only on an approved basis. |

Maintain an objection register with exact buyer wording, account/role, source/date, alternative, response used, evidence needed, outcome, and owner. Route recurring proof gaps to Positioning, Messaging, Competitive Intelligence, Content, Legal, or Delivery rather than letting sellers improvise.

### Acceptance and escalation

Delivery accepts within `[NEEDS INPUT: approved handoff SLA]` or returns a named blocker. The commercial owner resolves scope, terms, or client-commitment gaps; Delivery resolves capability and capacity gaps; Marketing Ops resolves fields/routing; Legal/Privacy resolves data and contractual questions. No silent scope expansion is allowed.

### Closed-loop feedback

Delivery returns weekly: work completed, client inputs received, approval delays, account progression, qualification quality, objections, data gaps, delivery risks, and recommendation. Sales Enablement updates the discovery guide, qualification rubric, proposal, and objection register only from recorded evidence.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The file names an accountable sales-enablement owner, commercial owner, delivery owner, and decision partners, with unknowns marked.
- The discovery guide captures account fit, trigger, problem, current process, implication, baseline, authority, alternatives, client responsibilities, constraints, and an agreed next step.
- The rubric distinguishes engagement, qualified conversation, proposal, and converted customer; it supports nurture and disqualification without forcing weak accounts into pipeline.
- The proposal narrative states the bounded sprint's scope, exclusions, client responsibilities, evidence plan, decision gate, and next step without invented pricing, proof, legal claims, or performance claims.
- Every objection response is a conversation frame with an evidence boundary and a named follow-up path; no competitor or status-quo alternative is misrepresented.
- The handoff packet contains the buyer's problem, evidence, stakeholders, scope, terms, owners, data boundaries, measurement fields, risks, and decision criteria.
- Sales, Delivery, Legal/Privacy, Marketing Ops, and Measurement acceptance points are explicit, and unresolved blockers cannot be silently passed downstream.
- All material unknowns appear as `[NEEDS INPUT]` or `Unknown`; no customer name, metric, price, certification, integration, local-access, legal, or outcome claim is invented.
- The enablement usage loop records what was used, in which conversation, with what buyer response, and what should be revised.
