# Workstream 12: Campaign Project Management

> **Status:** Execution brief for a first paid engagement. The operating model is a starting contract; client-specific scope, capacity, budget, dates, approvers, and systems remain `[NEEDS INPUT]` until confirmed.
>
> **Purpose:** Turn the GTM strategy and specialist workstreams into one controlled delivery plan with visible dependencies, explicit decisions, quality gates, and a documented scale, revise, or stop outcome.

## Objective

Orchestrate one bounded Saudi B2B acquisition engagement from approved scope through launch, learning, and continuation decision. Protect the client from uncoordinated activity and protect specialists from ambiguous briefs, hidden dependencies, late approvals, and scope creep.

The campaign coordinator owns the integrated plan and operating rhythm. Functional owners remain accountable for the quality and correctness of their specialist outputs. Project management does not replace strategy, channel expertise, sales ownership, legal review, or client decisions.

The first engagement must have one commercial objective, one primary audience or account motion, one primary conversion, a named client decision-maker, and an agreed end-of-engagement decision. Do not begin production until the scope and gate criteria are approved.

## Owner

**Accountable owner:** Campaign Project Manager / Campaign Coordinator.

**Required partners:** client executive sponsor; client day-to-day decision-maker; ABM owner; product marketing/positioning owner; messaging owner; content owner; SEO/AEO owner; social/outbound/email owners; analytics/marketing ops owner; performance analyst; sales owner; finance/procurement; `ops-legal-compliance`; Arabic reviewer; creative/web owner where applicable.

The project manager owns the integrated plan, dependency register, decision log, backlog, risk log, status dashboard, gate agendas, change control, and follow-up. A functional owner owns the acceptance and rework of that function's deliverable. The client approves scope, claims, budget, customer-facing assets, launch, and continuation.

No task is considered ready because an agent has been assigned. It must have an owner, due date, acceptance criterion, dependency status, and source brief.

## Dependency Management

Maintain one dependency register linked to the task backlog. A dependency is blocking when its absence prevents a task from starting, being approved, launching, or being measured; it is not merely useful context.

| Dependency class | Minimum evidence before dependent work proceeds | Owner | Escalation trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial | Approved offer, audience, CTA, price/process boundary, capacity, and qualification definition | Client sponsor + GTM owner | Missing at scope gate or changes after build begins |
| Evidence and claims | Source, date, permission, claim status, and approved proof or `[NEEDS INPUT]` | PMM + legal/compliance | Unsupported claim in a customer-facing asset |
| Audience and data | Deduplicated account/person IDs, role evidence, suppression, consent, and source date | ABM + Marketing Ops | Any send or upload without clearance |
| Creative/content | Approved brief, message, format, language reviewer, and asset owner | Content/design owner | Asset starts without a complete brief |
| Technical | CMS, forms, CRM fields, UTM/campaign ID, landing page, integrations, and QA environment | Marketing Ops + web owner | Test path cannot reach CRM or reporting |
| Sales | Named seller, handoff fields, response SLA, qualification outcome, and follow-up capacity | Sales owner | Positive signal unaccepted for one business day |
| Budget/procurement | Budget holder, approved spend, vendor access, and payment route | Client finance/procurement | Spend or vendor work is not authorized |
| Measurement | Event taxonomy, account ID, stage timestamps, attribution, dashboard owner, and reconciliation check | Analytics/Marketing Ops | Required completeness below 90% or unresolved identity issue |

For every dependency record: `ID | dependent task | required by | supplying owner | status | evidence/link | impact | next action | escalation date`. The project manager reviews blockers daily during build and launch, removes stale dependencies, and never silently marks an unknown as ready.

## Decision Rights

Use one decision log with `decision | options | recommendation | owner | approver | due date | rationale | impact | status`. Decisions are valid only when recorded.

| Decision | Recommends | Approves / decides | Consulted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign scope, objective, audience, and budget | Project manager + functional leads | Client executive sponsor | GTM owner, finance, sales |
| Positioning, message, and public claims | PMM/messaging owner | Client approver; legal for regulated or sensitive claims | Competitive intelligence, content, sales |
| Account list, tier, and outreach eligibility | ABM owner | Client sales/commercial owner; legal for channel restrictions | Project manager, Marketing Ops |
| Asset quality and readiness | Functional owner | Project manager for completeness; client approver for publication | Legal, Arabic reviewer, end-user reviewer |
| Consent, privacy, data use, and channel legality | Legal/compliance owner | Legal/compliance decision-maker | Marketing Ops, client sponsor |
| Launch timing and spend release | Project manager | Client sponsor after gate sign-off | Channel owners, finance, analytics |
| Sales acceptance and opportunity stage | Sales owner | Client commercial owner | ABM, Marketing Ops, project manager |
| Scope change and replan | Project manager | Client sponsor for cost/date/scope impact | Affected functional owners |
| Scale, revise, hold, or stop | Project manager + analyst | Client sponsor | All owners, sales, finance, delivery |

The project manager may pause work for an unmet quality, legal, data, or measurement gate. The client sponsor may reject or defer a recommendation, but the rationale and resulting risk must be logged.

## Cadence and Reporting

Use Asia/Riyadh for displayed deadlines and store system timestamps in UTC. Meeting notes, decisions, blockers, and owner/date actions are posted within one business day.

| Cadence | Participants | Purpose | Required output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily async during build/launch | All active owners | Status, next action, blocker, dependency change | Updated board and blocker list |
| 15-minute daily standup during launch | Project manager, channel owners, analytics, sales | Resolve same-day delivery, handoff, data, and spend issues | New blockers assigned with escalation dates |
| Weekly campaign huddle | Client decision-maker, project manager, all workstream leads | Review plan vs actual, risks, budget, pipeline movement, decisions, and next week | Dashboard, decision log, replan, and change/risk updates |
| Gate review | Required approvers only | Decide pass, conditional pass, hold, or fail | Signed gate decision and remediation owner/date |
| Sales/marketing review weekly | Sales, ABM, messaging, analytics, project manager | Review accepted handoffs, objections, qualification, and next milestones | Loss/objection loop and routing changes |
| Retrospective within 2 weeks of close | Full delivery team + client sponsor | Actual vs planned time, cost, scope, quality, and learning | Retrospective, lessons log, and next-cycle recommendations |

The weekly dashboard shows health (`on-track`, `at-risk`, `off-track`), planned vs completed tasks, critical-path dates, budget spent/remaining, top three risks, unresolved decisions, upcoming deadlines, accepted handoffs, data completeness, and the next gate.

## Phase Plan and Gates

Use the six CATALYST phases, compressed or expanded to fit the paid scope. The 20% contingency is reserved for rework, approvals, data fixes, and integration surprises; it is not pre-allocated to new deliverables.

| Phase | Primary work | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Discovery** | Confirm evidence boundary, client team, offer, audience, baseline, constraints, risks, and decision to proceed. | **Scope gate:** signed scope, accountable sponsor, access plan, budget, and `[NEEDS INPUT]` register accepted. |
| **1. Strategy** | Align ICP/ABM, positioning, competitive context, messaging, channel motion, offer, qualification, and measurement plan. | **Strategy gate:** one coherent brief, approved hypothesis, claims boundary, account/motion choice, and success/stop criteria. |
| **2. Foundation** | Configure backlog, owners, account IDs, suppression, CRM fields, campaign taxonomy, analytics events, approvals, and production workspace. | **Readiness gate:** critical dependencies cleared, test records pass end to end, consent/legal path approved, and dashboard is usable. |
| **3. Build** | Produce and review assets, sequences, landing pages, account briefs, sales enablement, QA evidence, and launch runbook. | **Build gate:** deliverables meet acceptance criteria, no unresolved critical defects, copy/design/legal/language sign-offs recorded. |
| **4. Launch** | Release the approved motion, monitor delivery, respond to signals, protect suppression, and record anomalies and handoffs. | **Launch gate:** tracking and handoffs work, no material compliance issue, owners meet SLAs, and the first review window is scheduled. |
| **5. Optimize** | Compare mature cohorts, update backlog by evidence, reallocate effort within scope, document learning, and recommend scale/revise/stop. | **Continuation gate:** client receives decision memo with evidence, uncertainty, budget/resource impact, and next approved action. |

Gate statuses are `pass`, `conditional pass`, `hold`, or `fail`. A conditional pass must name a bounded remediation, owner, due date, and whether launch is permitted. A failed gate stops dependent work until the decision log records a new disposition.

## Backlog Governance

The backlog is the single ordered list for tasks, experiments, defects, decisions, and change requests. Every item includes `ID | type | outcome | owner | estimate | dependency | acceptance criterion | priority | status | due date | evidence | next review`.

Prioritize with this order:

1. Legal, privacy, consent, safety, or data-integrity blockers.
2. Critical-path work required for the next gate or launch.
3. Work that improves measurement or unblocks a high-confidence commercial test.
4. Client-approved deliverables tied to the scope objective.
5. Experiments with a stated disconfirming check and low rework cost.
6. Nice-to-have polish, additional channels, and speculative content.

Use `now`, `next`, `later`, `blocked`, and `parked`. Review `now` and `blocked` items weekly. Do not add work directly to `now` without removing, delaying, or re-estimating another item. New requests are either within an existing deliverable, a tradeoff, or a formal change request; “quick extras” do not bypass the queue.

Each experiment must state hypothesis, audience, variant, owner, minimum evidence, observation window, disconfirming result, and decision date. Close completed items with an artifact link and acceptance record; do not leave “done” as a narrative status.

## Risk, Issue, and Escalation Management

Maintain a living log with `ID | risk/issue | probability | impact | score | trigger | mitigation | contingency | owner | due date | status | escalation path`. Review it in every weekly huddle and immediately for a critical issue.

Escalate when:

- a critical-path task is forecast to slip by more than two business days or the gate by more than three to five days;
- a decision is overdue beyond the agreed client SLA;
- a dependency has no owner or remains blocked for two working days;
- scope, budget, or resource demand changes materially;
- data completeness falls below 90%, identity/suppression is uncertain, or attribution breaks;
- a claim, translation, audience, or channel raises a legal/compliance concern;
- a positive signal is not accepted within one business day;
- delivery capacity, margin, or client access makes the approved promise impractical.

Escalation path: functional owner -> project manager -> client day-to-day decision-maker -> client executive sponsor. Legal/privacy issues go directly to the legal decision-maker and pause the affected activity. The escalation record names the issue, impact, options, recommendation, decision deadline, and communication owner.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The signed scope names one objective, audience/motion, conversion, timeline, budget, deliverable set, client responsibilities, contingency, and exit decision.
- The integrated plan is capacity-realistic, identifies the critical path and all blocking dependencies, and shows 20% contingency without hiding scope.
- Every active backlog item, dependency, risk, decision, and change has an owner, date, status, acceptance criterion, and evidence or next action.
- Decision rights and escalation contacts are explicit; overdue decisions and schedule/budget risks are surfaced within the agreed SLA.
- No customer-facing asset, audience activation, paid spend, or launch proceeds without the relevant content, claims, language, legal, suppression, technical, and measurement gate records.
- Handoffs are accepted by named downstream owners with the required fields, source links, SLA, and next milestone; unaccepted work remains open.
- The dashboard distinguishes planned vs actual work, budget, account/funnel progression, primary-source attribution, data completeness, and immature cohorts.
- Scope additions are logged as changes with impact and an approved tradeoff or replan; no silent scope creep remains.
- The final decision memo states scale, revise, hold, or stop, cites the evidence and uncertainty, reconciles actual time/cost/scope, and identifies the next owner and date.
