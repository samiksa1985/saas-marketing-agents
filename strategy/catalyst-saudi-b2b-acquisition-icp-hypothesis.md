# CATALYST Saudi B2B Acquisition ICP Hypothesis

> **Status:** Working hypothesis for the master GTM brief, not validated research.
>
> **Evidence boundary:** No company brand context, customer evidence, market statistics, proof points, or named accounts were supplied. All claims below are assumptions to test. Do not use them as factual market claims or public proof.

## Strategic Read

The likely best first customer is not simply a Saudi SME that wants more leads. It is a B2B company with a commercially urgent growth target, a founder or revenue leader still close to pipeline, and enough delivery capacity and sales discipline to convert incremental demand. The offer should be framed as an **outsourced acquisition department**: coordinated strategy, demand creation, lifecycle follow-up, measurement, and learning, with clear client-side ownership.

The primary research question is: **Which Saudi B2B companies experience acquisition as a consequential operating gap, have authority and budget to outsource it, and can prove or observe commercial outcomes quickly enough for a first engagement to earn trust?**

## Working ICP

### Firmographic fit

- **Geography:** Saudi Arabia; prioritize companies selling inside the Kingdom before testing GCC expansion.
- **Company stage:** Established small business or mid-market company with a functioning offer, references or repeat business, and an active sales motion. Do not use a headcount or revenue cutoff until local data establishes one.
- **Commercial model:** B2B, with a defined buyer role, non-trivial consideration, and a sales-assisted purchase. Favor recurring, repeatable, project-based, or contract-led revenue over one-off consumer transactions.
- **Vertical hypothesis:** Start where expertise, trust, tender/vendor qualification, and relationship-led selling matter: business services, industrial services/suppliers, technology/information services, logistics, facilities/technical services, and professional services. Treat this as a test set, not a claim that all are equally attractive.
- **Market position:** A credible specialist or challenger that can win on a clear use case, service level, expertise, speed, or local relevance, rather than only on lowest price.
- **Growth posture:** Has a stated growth, market-entry, utilization, or pipeline objective that senior leadership reviews.

### Operational fit

Strong-fit accounts are likely to have:

- One accountable commercial owner, even if there is no formal marketing department.
- At least one person who can supply subject-matter input, approve messaging, and follow up with sales opportunities.
- A documented or observable sales process: lead capture, qualification, meetings, proposals, and close/loss tracking.
- A CRM or at minimum a recoverable record of prospects, customers, proposals, and outcomes. A spreadsheet is not disqualifying; zero visibility is.
- A website, social presence, referral base, event activity, or customer list that can seed learning and distribution.
- Delivery capacity to accept qualified demand without damaging service quality.
- Willingness to share commercial data and make decisions on a weekly operating cadence.

### Buying triggers

Prioritize accounts where one or more of these events is active:

1. Revenue target increased, but the current referral or relationship network is insufficient.
2. A new service, product line, branch, or Saudi market segment needs a repeatable pipeline.
3. Founder or senior sales leadership is still sourcing and chasing most opportunities.
4. An internal marketer left, is overloaded, or produces activity without pipeline accountability.
5. Sales capacity exists but is underutilized, or delivery utilization is uneven.
6. A tender, partnership, event, launch, or strategic account push creates a time-bound demand need.
7. The company has tried agencies, freelancers, paid media, or internal hiring and cannot connect activity to qualified opportunities.
8. Leadership wants an in-house growth function but cannot yet justify or staff the full department.

## Priority Segments

| Segment | Situation and job | Likely buying owner | Why it may be a wedge | Main disqualifier to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder-led specialist B2B** | Turn founder/referral-led growth into a repeatable pipeline without hiring a full team. | Founder/CEO, often with a sales or operations lead. | Short decision path; pain is visible; direct access to voice and proof. | Founder will not delegate access, approve quickly, or share data. |
| **Commercially ready mid-market** | Coordinate disconnected marketing, sales, and follow-up around a defined revenue target. | GM/Managing Director, CRO/Head of Sales, or commercial director. | Larger impact and clearer operating sponsor; potential for a sustained department mandate. | Existing agency or internal team owns the work and will not collaborate. |
| **New-offer or market-entry unit** | Create demand and validate positioning for a new offer, vertical, branch, or Saudi expansion motion. | Business-unit leader, GM, or product/commercial owner. | Trigger creates urgency and a bounded learning brief. | Offer, pricing, delivery, or target buyer is still materially undefined. |
| **Capacity-constrained service operator** | Fill qualified demand into available delivery capacity while protecting lead quality. | Owner, COO, or commercial lead. | The value conversation can connect acquisition to utilization, not vanity traffic. | No capacity, no differentiation, or inability to respond to leads. |

**Segmenting rule:** Use firmographics for account selection, but use **trigger + operating readiness + buying authority** for prioritization. Two companies of the same size may have opposite fit if one has an urgent commercial owner and the other only wants “more awareness.”

## Pain and Jobs-to-be-Done Hypothesis

**Functional job:** Build a dependable acquisition system that turns a defined B2B offer into qualified conversations, with the handoff and measurement needed to learn what converts.

**Emotional job:** Reduce the anxiety of an unpredictable pipeline and stop making visible growth decisions from anecdotes, channel activity, or founder instinct alone.

**Social job:** Help the owner or commercial leader appear capable of scaling the company without immediately building a large internal marketing department.

Likely pains to validate, in the customer's language:

- “Marketing” is fragmented across people and vendors; nobody owns the full path to revenue.
- Lead volume is not the same as qualified opportunity, and sales distrusts marketing activity.
- The company knows its service but cannot express a sharp buyer problem or differentiated reason to choose it.
- Follow-up is inconsistent because sales leaders are busy, leads arrive in multiple channels, or ownership is unclear.
- The business cannot tell which channels, messages, or segments deserve more investment.
- Hiring a full department feels premature, but project-by-project outsourcing creates coordination overhead.
- Arabic/English language, local trust, procurement, relationship, or compliance requirements may affect conversion. These are discovery topics, not assumed barriers or differentiators.

## Buyer Committee

- **Economic buyer:** Founder/CEO in smaller firms; GM/Managing Director or commercial executive in mid-market firms. Cares about growth confidence, risk, cash discipline, and control.
- **Champion:** Head of Sales, business development lead, commercial director, or ambitious operations leader. Feels the pipeline gap daily and can define the first use case.
- **User/operator:** Client-side marketer, coordinator, sales development, or subject-matter expert who supplies inputs and handles follow-up. Needs simple ownership, usable outputs, and low coordination burden.
- **Influencers/blockers:** Finance for budget and payback logic; procurement/legal for vendor onboarding and contracting; IT/data or compliance where access, privacy, systems, or communications create risk; delivery/operations if new demand could exceed capacity.

**Committee hypothesis:** The sale will stall when the champion wants activity but the economic buyer cannot see a bounded commercial outcome, or when no client-side operator is available to execute the handoff. The offer needs a named sponsor, a named operator, a defined first motion, and an agreed evidence review.

## Anti-ICP

Deprioritize or disqualify accounts that:

- Sell primarily B2C, have an undifferentiated commodity offer, or compete almost entirely on lowest price.
- Have no clear buyer, offer owner, sales process, delivery capacity, or willingness to follow up.
- Want guaranteed revenue, instant leads, mass lead volume, or a “set and forget” vendor.
- Cannot provide access to decision-makers, subject-matter input, customer/CRM evidence, or basic conversion data.
- Are pre-offer, pre-delivery, or still searching for product-market fit rather than an acquisition system.
- Require highly bespoke procurement, enterprise integration, or regulatory work before a bounded pilot can start.
- Expect the outsourced team to replace sales, account management, delivery, or executive decision-making.

## First Wedge Recommendation

**Test first:** a time-bounded **pipeline reset and acquisition sprint** for founder-led specialist B2B firms and commercially ready mid-market units with one defined offer and one buyer segment.

Scope the wedge around:

- Clarifying the offer, buyer, and qualification definition.
- Selecting one acquisition motion, initially based on where the account already has access or signal: existing relationships/customer list, targeted outbound, search intent, partner/event follow-up, or a focused content/social motion.
- Installing a simple lead-to-opportunity operating loop: capture, qualification, owner, response SLA, meeting outcome, proposal/loss reason, and weekly review.
- Producing the minimum message, campaign, sales handoff, and reporting assets needed to learn.
- Ending with a decision: scale, revise the segment/offer, or stop.

Do not lead with “we do all marketing.” Lead with **one commercial problem, one buyer, one operating owner, and one evidence loop**. The durable expansion path, if validated, is the outsourced department mandate after the first motion proves that the client can collaborate and convert.

**Initial channel posture:** Do not pre-commit to a channel mix. Choose the first motion from customer access, buying behavior, sales capacity, language requirements, compliance, and measurable handoff quality. Validate whether Arabic, English, or bilingual execution is required rather than treating language as a marketing flourish.

## Assumptions to Validate

- Saudi B2B SMEs and mid-market leaders will buy coordinated acquisition ownership, not only isolated execution.
- The pain is urgent enough to fund before a full internal department is hired.
- A bounded sprint can show useful leading and commercial evidence without promising a guaranteed outcome.
- The target company can provide enough data, access, and response capacity for the service to work.
- The best first segment is defined more by trigger and readiness than by industry or size alone.
- Local trust, procurement, language, and relationship dynamics materially change the buying and delivery model.
- The service can differentiate from agencies, freelancers, internal hiring, and status quo through coordination and accountability; this must be proven, not asserted.
- One repeatable acquisition motion can be learned before expanding to a multi-channel department.

## Ten Highest-Value Discovery Questions

1. What commercial event made acquisition a priority now, and what happens if it is not solved this quarter?
2. Walk us through the last qualified B2B opportunity: how was it sourced, who handled it, how long did it take, and why was it won or lost?
3. What percentage of current opportunities comes from referrals, relationships, tenders, partners, outbound, search, events, or other sources? What is known versus guessed?
4. Which offer has enough margin, delivery capacity, and differentiation to be the first acquisition wedge?
5. Who owns pipeline, who owns marketing activity, and who is accountable when a lead is not followed up?
6. What does “qualified” mean to sales, and which lead types are actively unwanted?
7. What has the company already tried with agencies, freelancers, hiring, paid channels, events, or content? What specifically failed and why?
8. Who must approve an external acquisition department, what evidence do they require, and what procurement, finance, privacy, or contracting constraints apply?
9. Can the team provide customer/prospect records, loss reasons, response history, offer materials, and weekly access to the decision-maker and operator?
10. What would make the first 30-90 days a credible success, and which outcome would make the engagement a clear stop?

## Validation Plan and Handoff

Run discovery across the four segments above, deliberately including non-buyers and companies that use alternatives. Compare trigger strength, pain severity, committee access, operating readiness, willingness to share evidence, and first-wedge repeatability. Do not report market size or segment prevalence until sourced.

**Minimum evidence for promotion into the master GTM brief:** recurring patterns from interviews and account reviews, documented disqualifiers, a named initial segment, a validated trigger, the buyer's own success language, and a first motion that can be measured through opportunity quality rather than lead volume alone.
