# First 90 Days: Saudi B2B SME and Mid-Market ABM Motion

## Audience and Decision

**Audience:** founding team of a Saudi-focused B2B customer acquisition service.

**Owner:** ABM strategist, with one named founder or seller paired to every Tier 1 and Tier 2 account.

**Decision enabled:** which named Saudi accounts receive coordinated attention during the first 90 days, what each account should experience, and whether the motion is earning more capacity.

This is a pilot motion, not a claim that the service has product-market fit. It is designed for a team with no customer history, limited delivery capacity, and no permission to invent account facts.

**Hard gate:** no account enters Tier 1 or Tier 2 execution without a named owner, a documented reason for selection, a next milestone, and a suppression/consent check. If sales cannot assign the owner, the account stays in research or is removed.

## Capacity Model

Start with attention, not a logo wish list.

For each seller:

```text
monthly account capacity = available selling hours
                           / (research + touches + follow-up + meeting preparation hours per account)
```

Use a one-week time audit before publishing the list. The pilot default is:

| Tier | Pilot count | Workload | Service level |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Tier 1, 1:1 | 4 | 60-90 minutes of research and preparation per account per cycle; bespoke point of view; weekly review | Named founder/seller; weekly account review |
| Tier 2, 1:few | 8 | 20-30 minutes of research per account; cluster message plus one account-specific line; monthly review | Named owner; monthly review |
| Tier 3, 1:many | 8 | Fit-qualified, light personalization, shared content and event/partner coverage | Quarterly review; no bespoke asset |

If the time audit supports only 12 accounts, publish 12. Cut the lowest-scoring accounts and record them in a rejected/parked list; do not keep 20 accounts and quietly reduce the agreed service level.

## Account Selection

Because there are no closed-won patterns, use a temporary evidence model and label it as a hypothesis. Score each account from 0-2 on each dimension; a zero in a disqualifying dimension removes the account.

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ICP fit | Outside stated market | Plausible SME/mid-market fit | Clearly matches serviceable segment and geography | Official site, company profile, supplied brief |
| Problem visibility | No observable acquisition problem | General growth or commercial activity | Specific public trigger connected to acquisition | Dated page, post, job, event, interview note |
| Reachability | No relevant contact or route | One plausible contact | Two or more relevant roles or a warm route | Public professional profile, partner, referral |
| Economic ability | Unknown | Plausible but unverified | Evidence of active commercial investment or buying process | Public hiring/initiative or direct conversation; never infer revenue |
| Strategic value | No reason beyond logo preference | Useful cluster or learning value | Strong reference, partner, or repeatable use-case hypothesis | Written rationale, not reputation |
| Timing | No dated trigger | Trigger older than 90 days | Trigger inside 60 days or confirmed in conversation | Source and date |

**Entry rule:** Tier 1 requires at least 8/12, two independent signal families, and a named owner. Tier 2 requires at least 6/12, one current signal plus reachable contact, and a named owner. Tier 3 requires ICP fit and a valid route to market. A score is a prioritization aid, not proof of need.

Every ledger row must contain:

```text
Account | URL | city/market | segment evidence | reason selected | source URLs/notes
signal family + date | tier | owner | committee contacts | stage | last touch
next milestone + date | suppression status | confidence | retirement date
```

Do not populate headcount, technology, funding, initiatives, competitors, customer names, or decision-maker names from intuition. Use `[NEEDS INPUT: ...]` until sourced.

## Initial Account Research Protocol

Run this protocol in Days 1-10. It is intentionally cheap enough for a founding team.

1. **Define the serviceable market.** Write the geographic boundary, employee/revenue proxy if used, sectors served, average contract value, delivery constraints, exclusions, Arabic/English capability, and minimum buying conditions. Mark every unknown.
2. **Build a 60-account candidate pool.** Use founder network, existing CRM/export if any, Saudi business directories, chambers/industry groups, partner lists, conference sponsors, public company sites, LinkedIn company search, and relevant job/event pages. Record source and access date for each candidate.
3. **De-duplicate and classify.** Normalize legal/company names, domains, subsidiaries, parent relationships, city, sector, and status. Remove existing customers, active opportunities, direct competitors, and accounts under suppression. Route installed-base expansion to customer marketing rather than this motion.
4. **Run a 10-minute desk research pass.** Capture only: what the company sells, evidence it serves B2B buyers, a dated commercial/growth trigger, two possible buying roles, and one reason the service might be relevant. Stop when the evidence is absent; do not manufacture a hypothesis as fact.
5. **Verify through a human signal.** Ask a founder, partner, or relevant contact one neutral discovery question. Example: "How are you currently creating qualified conversations with the companies you want to sell to?" Treat the answer as evidence only when recorded with date and source.
6. **Score and tier.** Apply the temporary model above, write a one-sentence rationale, and mark confidence as high, medium, or low. Select the 20 highest-confidence accounts within capacity; keep the rest parked.
7. **Map the committee.** Research role before name: economic buyer, commercial owner, marketing/demand owner, operational/service owner, finance/procurement, and technical/privacy reviewer where relevant. Mark each role known, hypothesized, or missing.
8. **Run a data and consent check.** Confirm whether outreach is permitted for the person and channel under the applicable Saudi and cross-border rules. Obtain the current suppression record from `ops-legal-compliance`; do not maintain a competing local opt-out list.
9. **Quality review.** A second founder reviews the top 20 for evidence quality, duplication, conflict, owner availability, and whether the proposed next milestone is a real business step.

### Research stop conditions

Park an account when two research passes produce no dated trigger, no reachable relevant role, or no credible reason to believe the problem is timely. Retire it when the account opts out, is already in an active sales conversation owned elsewhere, is not serviceable, or has no progression after the declared review window.

## Tier Design and Personalization

### Tier 1: Strategic, 1:1

Four accounts maximum in the pilot. Build a one-page account brief: business model, sourced trigger, opportunity hypothesis, committee map, likely buying criteria, risks, relevant proof, and next milestone. Personalization means changing the problem hypothesis and proposed conversation, not inserting the company name into a generic email.

Use founder-to-founder or senior commercial outreach, one useful diagnostic or teardown, a tailored conversation agenda, and a joint success hypothesis. No bespoke case study or free consulting deliverable before a qualified conversation.

### Tier 2: Clustered, 1:few

Eight accounts grouped by one observable shared context, such as sector, sales motion, or acquisition constraint. Create one cluster insight and one proof/diagnostic asset; add one sourced account line per message. Each account gets a primary and backup contact, but not a bespoke landing page.

### Tier 3: Programmatic, 1:many

Eight accounts receive shared educational or event content, partner distribution, and light founder visibility. Personalization is limited to sector, role, or use case. Do not promise account-specific research or a sales SLA that the team cannot meet.

Re-tier only at Day 30, 60, and 90, or after a material commercial change. Promote on progression plus corroborated signals; demote on no response/progression after two cycles, weak evidence, or owner capacity loss. Exceptions such as a strategic partner must be logged with an approver and expiry date.

## Buying Committee Map

For every Tier 1 and Tier 2 account, maintain this map:

| Role | What to learn | Entry hypothesis | Coverage status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Commercial outcome, budget, risk tolerance | Founder/GM/CEO in smaller firms; `[NEEDS INPUT]` per account | Known / hypothesized / missing |
| Commercial owner | Pipeline, sales productivity, channel mix | Head of Sales/Business Development | Known / hypothesized / missing |
| Marketing owner | Demand quality, attribution, content and campaign workload | Marketing lead or growth owner | Known / hypothesized / missing |
| Delivery/operations | Capacity to fulfill new demand and quality constraints | Operations/service leader | Known / hypothesized / missing |
| Finance/procurement | Vendor setup, payment terms, approval path | Finance or procurement contact | Known / hypothesized / missing |
| Technical/privacy reviewer | Data access, integrations, security, consent | `[NEEDS INPUT: whether service requires access or integration]` | Known / hypothesized / missing |

The first meeting should identify the decision process, not pitch every role. The seller owns the map; marketing supplies research and assets. A Tier 1 account is not "covered" because one champion replied: require at least two relevant roles engaged before calling the committee covered.

## Signals to Actions

| Signal family | Freshness | Minimum corroboration | Action | Owner / SLA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-party: reply, meeting, detailed question | 30 days | One direct signal is enough to respond; do not treat it as buying intent | Human response and discovery scheduling | Paired seller, same business day |
| First-party: repeat site/content engagement | 21 days | Combine with a role/contact or external trigger | Send the relevant cluster insight; invite a conversation | Founder/marketing, 2 business days |
| Commercial: open opportunity or active sales conversation | Current status | No second signal needed for suppression | Stop ABM automation; hand to deal owner | ABM strategist immediately |
| External: dated hiring, expansion, launch, event, partnership, or procurement notice | 60 days | Combine with fit plus a reachable role | Create a trigger-led conversation hypothesis | Research owner, 3 business days |
| Partner/referral introduction | 90 days | Confirm consent and relevance | Warm introduction with agreed context | Partner/founder, 2 business days |
| Third-party intent score | 14 days | Never acts alone; pair with first-party or commercial signal | Research, do not launch a high-effort play yet | ABM strategist, 3 business days |

Undated signals have no action value. Two signals must come from independent families before a Tier 1 bespoke asset, executive outreach, or event investment. Suppress all plays for opt-outs, active conversations, known customer accounts, legal restrictions, or a recent touch inside the agreed cooling-off period.

## 90-Day Orchestration Contract

### Days 1-30: Learn and earn permission

| Order | Touch | Tier | Owner | Dependency |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm introduction request or permission-based first contact | 1/2 | Founder/seller | Verified role, source, and suppression check |
| 2 | Useful cluster insight or account-specific observation | 1/2/3 | Marketing + PMM | Evidence review and approved message |
| 3 | Phone/LinkedIn follow-up where permitted | 1/2 | Seller | No opt-out; no active conversation |
| 4 | Discovery conversation with a defined next milestone | 1/2 | Seller | Contact accepts; agenda sent |
| 5 | Shared roundtable, partner session, or office-hours invitation | 2/3 | Founder/marketing | Event owner, consent, and capacity |

### Days 31-60: Test the offer and committee depth

Tier 1 receives one tailored diagnostic and an executive-level follow-up only after the account confirms the problem. Tier 2 receives the winning cluster message and one role-specific variation. Tier 3 receives one shared asset or event touch. Every touch records account stage, role engaged, response, objection, and next date.

### Days 61-90: Convert learning into a buying step

For engaged accounts, the seller proposes one concrete milestone: qualified discovery, scoped pilot, proposal review, or mutual action plan. For unengaged accounts, make one final low-pressure value touch, then pause for the retirement review. Do not extend a sequence merely because the 90-day window is ending.

### Handoffs

| Condition | Handoff | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Positive reply or meeting requested | `sales-outbound-strategist` / paired seller | Owner accepts within one business day and logs next step |
| Active opportunity or live sales conversation | `sales-deal-strategist` | ABM automation suppressed; deal owner confirms handoff |
| Message or proof gap | `pmm-messaging-architect` | One approved hypothesis and evidence-backed message |
| Audience, routing, field, or scoring need | `analytics-marketing-ops-architect` | Ledger fields and routing rule documented |
| Consent, opt-out, or channel legality question | `ops-legal-compliance` | Suppression/permission status resolved before touch |
| Paid audience request | `paid-media-social-ads-specialist` | Audience size, match rate, frequency, and platform feasibility confirmed |

Paid media is not assumed for the pilot. A small Tier 1 list cannot independently satisfy LinkedIn's 300-row company audience floor; use founder outreach, partners, events, and organic distribution unless the broader compliant audience is large enough and paid media confirms execution.

## Measurement and Scoreboard

Create one ledger and report the same account IDs across CRM, outreach, events, and paid systems.

| Metric | Definition / query | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence completeness | Accounts with rationale, source/date, tier, owner, and next milestone / active accounts | Weekly |
| Capacity adherence | Active accounts <= documented capacity; list every parked/cut account | Weekly |
| Account coverage | Target accounts with at least one known relevant role engaged in trailing 30 days / target accounts | Weekly by tier |
| Committee depth | Tier 1 accounts with 2+ relevant roles engaged / Tier 1 accounts with any engagement | Monthly |
| Account progression | Count and share moving Unaware -> Aware -> Engaged -> Qualified conversation -> Opportunity | Weekly |
| Penetration | Target accounts with an open opportunity / target accounts | Monthly |
| First-contact yield | Accounts with first meaningful reply or meeting by first-touch channel | Monthly |
| Handoff speed | Time from positive signal to accepted seller next step | Weekly |
| Pipeline | CRM pipeline from named target accounts, reported by primary source | Monthly |
| Learning velocity | Documented objections, buying criteria, and message tests completed | Weekly |

For Days 1-90, progression, committee depth, coverage, and accepted handoffs are leading evidence. Win-rate and cycle-length deltas require a published comparison set of similar non-target accounts and enough time to mature; do not manufacture a benchmark.

**Credit rule:** one primary source per opportunity. ABM reports account progression and assist touches; it does not add a second copy of pipeline already credited to outbound, paid, partner, event, or inbound. Use a pre/post list read or a matched holdout when measurement volume permits. Route attribution implementation to `paid-media-attribution-analyst`.

**Do not track as success metrics:** MQL volume, raw impressions, contact count without account context, or "influenced pipeline" summed across channels.

## Review Gates

### Day 10: list gate

Approve or reject the 20-account pilot. Confirm capacity, account reasons, owner assignment, suppression, and research confidence. Any missing item parks the account.

### Day 30: signal gate

Keep, promote, demote, or retire based on progression and corroborated signals. Review message objections and whether the offer is understandable. No progression is not failure by itself, but no learning and no owner is a retirement case.

### Day 60: economics gate

Compare founder time, meetings, qualified conversations, and account progression by tier. Stop bespoke work that has not produced learning or a credible next step. Reallocate freed hours to the strongest cluster.

### Day 90: continuation gate

Continue, redesign, or stop the motion. Continue only where the team can name: the best-fit cluster, the trigger that creates urgency, the role that responds, the next commercial milestone, and the capacity required to serve it. Record all parked accounts and the evidence needed to reconsider them.

## First-90-Day Output Pack

- Account ledger with source/date, score, tier, owner, stage, next milestone, suppression status, and confidence.
- Research log containing URLs, access dates, contact-role evidence, and rejected-account reasons.
- Four Tier 1 account briefs and eight Tier 2 cluster/account cards, or fewer if capacity requires.
- Buying-committee map with gaps and handoff status.
- Three dated touch plans, one per tier, with dependencies and cooling-off rules.
- Weekly scoreboard and Day 30, 60, and 90 decisions.
- Retirement and parked-account register.

The first motion's job is not to look like a mature enterprise ABM program. Its job is to discover which Saudi B2B segment, trigger, buyer role, and offer combination can earn the next unit of founder attention.
